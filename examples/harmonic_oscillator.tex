% The energy of a classical harmonic oscillator does not depend on time.
Take $x = A\cos(\omega t + \phi)$ and $v = -A\omega\sin(\omega t + \phi)$, with
$\omega = \sqrt{k/m}$ and $k, m > 0$. The total energy is
\begin{align}
  E &= \frac{1}{2} m v^2 + \frac{1}{2} k x^2 \\
    &= \frac{1}{2} m A^2 \omega^2 \sin^2(\omega t + \phi)
       + \frac{1}{2} k A^2 \cos^2(\omega t + \phi) \\
    &= \frac{1}{2} k A^2 ,
\end{align}
which does not depend on $t$.
